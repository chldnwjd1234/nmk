Collectors of Light

Nov-14-2025 ~ Mar-15-2026 | Special Exhibition Gallery 1

Exhibition Introduction

This exhibition showcases French masterpieces from Impressionism to Early Modernism, collected by American banker Robert Lehman. Once dismissed for their vivid colors and bold brushwork, these works later became the gateway to Modernism, illuminating how artists captured light and transformed art’s course.

Exhibit

81 objects from the Metropolitan Museum of Art,including Auguste Renoir, Two Young Girls at the Piano, Vincent van Gogh, The Flowering Orchard, Mary Cassatt,
Spring: Margot Standing in a Garden.

Reservation

Collectors of Light

From Impressionism to early Modernism,
artists redefined vision itself through the study of light.

The Collectors of Light presents 81 works from
The Met’s Robert Lehman Collection,
celebrating both the painters who captured light
and the collector who devoted his life to preserving it.

Once seen as radical, Impressionism became
the language that opened the path to modern art —
inviting us to reflect on how the act of seeing transforms creation.

"The Collectors of Light" at The Met showcases 81 works from the Robert Lehman Collection, tracing how Impressionist and early Modernist artists redefined vision through light — and how one collector preserved their legacy.

Exhibition Structure

01

The Human Body, Human Nature

This section explores how depictions of the human body—long at the center of art—shifted in the 19th century toward a human-centered way of thinking.

This section shows how 19th-century art reimagined the human body with a new human-centered vision.

Paul Cézanne
(The Bathers / Les Baigneuses)
02

Ourselves Today: Portraits and Individuality

Focusing on 19th-century portraiture that reflected both individuality and social identity, this section highlights the diverse faces and characters that defined the era.

This section highlights 19th-century portraits that captured individuality and social identity.

Mary Cassatt
(Spring: Margot Standing in a Garden)
03

Eternal Moments in Nature

Amid the transformations of industrialization, artists sought new ways to capture nature. This section examines their attempts to portray landscapes encountered directly outdoors from fresh perspectives.

This section explores how artists redefined nature through fresh, outdoor perspectives.

Auguste Renoir
(Figures on the Beach)
04

Ourselves Today: Portraits and Individuality

Focusing on 19th-century portraiture that reflected both individuality and social identity, this section highlights the diverse faces and characters that defined the era.

This section highlights portraits capturing 19th-century individuality and identity.

Mary Cassatt
(Spring: Margot Standing in a Garden)
05

Reflections in Rippling Water

Through riverside and waterside scenes that allowed for experiments with light and color, this section sheds light on the Impressionists’ innovative approaches to perception and representation.

This section explores how riverside scenes inspired Impressionists’ experiments with light and color.

Vincent van Gogh
(Flowering Orchard)
